Project 1 · Demand planning

FMCG Demand & Supply Planning

Three years of daily sales for 30 SKUs, used to answer the questions a demand planner faces every month: when demand peaks, which forecast to trust, how much promos add, and what stockouts cost.

Data: FMCG Daily Sales Data 2022–2024 (Kaggle, beatafaron), a public synthetic dataset. The results demonstrate the method; they are not findings about a real company.

The questions

  1. When does demand peak, and does every category peak at the same time?
  2. Which simple forecast method is accurate enough to plan with?
  3. How much do promotions lift demand, and how much more variable does it get?
  4. How much demand is lost when stock runs out?
  5. How do new SKUs ramp up after launch?

Data preparation

One row is one SKU, one day, one channel (3), one region (3), and one pack type. Period: 21 Jan 2022 to 31 Dec 2024.

  • Removed 3 rows with negative units sold (190,757 → 190,754). No duplicates or missing values.
  • Checked the data card against the file. Two claims did not hold (new SKUs only in 2024; rising prices), so neither is used.
  • Every step is logged in the workbook's CLEANING_LOG sheet.

Monthly units sold, all SKUs

Units per month, Jan 2022 – Dec 2024 (Jan 2022 starts on the 21st)

The jump from 2022 to 2023 comes from new SKUs (20 active in 2022, 30 from 2023), not from existing products selling more. Units in 2024 were 0.9% below 2023. Reading this chart as "consistent growth" would overstate demand.

1 · Seasonality: not every category peaks together

Seasonality index by month, 2023–2024. Dashed line = 100, the average month.

Total demand peaks in June–July (index 114) and bottoms out in December (82). Juice swings the most (peak 138 in June, low 64 in December). ReadyMeal runs the opposite way: it peaks in February (121) when other categories are low. If ReadyMeal shares a production line with summer categories, this pattern helps level capacity across the year.

2 · Forecast accuracy: no single best method

MAPE of three monthly forecasts, backtested on the 12 months of 2024 (lower is better)

At total level, last month's value (naive) is the most accurate at 5.9%. By category it changes: seasonal naive wins for Yogurt (5.8%) and ReadyMeal (5.7%), naive wins for Milk, SnackBar, and Juice. Seasonal naive fails for Milk and SnackBar because many of their SKUs launched in 2023, so last year's level is not comparable. A planner should pick the method per category, not one method for everything.

3 · Promotions nearly double demand, and its variability

Average units per row, with and without promotion

14.9% of rows are on promotion. Promo rows sell 34.1 units on average against 17.4 without (+95%), and the uplift is consistent across categories (+93% to +97%). The standard deviation of demand also doubles, so safety stock for promo periods needs to be roughly twice the normal level.

4 · Stockouts: estimated lost sales by SKU

Top 10 SKUs by estimated lost units, 2022–2024

2.0% of rows have zero stock, and every one of them sells zero units. Estimating what those rows would have sold (the average for the same SKU, channel, region, and promo status in the same month while stock was available) gives about 78,400 lost units, or 2.0% of demand, worth about 411,800 in dataset currency units. Stockout rates are narrow across SKUs (1.8% to 2.5%), and delivery lead time (1–5 days) shows no effect on sales or stockouts.

5 · New SKU launch curve

Units per row by month since launch, indexed to each SKU's stable level (dashed line = 100, its average for months 13–24). 20 SKUs launched Jun 2022 – May 2023.

New SKUs sell about 87% of their stable level in months 1–2, then spike to 126–137% in months 3–6, ease to 104–107% in months 8–12, and settle near 100% in the second year. A launch plan built on the first two months would under-supply the spike that follows. Limitation: launch months differ, so some seasonality is mixed into this curve.

What a planner would do with this

  • Build the production plan around category-specific seasonality, and use ReadyMeal's opposite cycle to level line capacity.
  • Forecast each category with the method that won its backtest, and re-run the backtest each quarter.
  • Double safety stock for SKUs entering a promotion.
  • Plan launch volumes for a month 3–6 spike, not for the month 1–2 level.

Left out on purpose

  • Delivered quantity: deliveries are about 9× sales while stock does not build up, so the stock flow is not consistent.
  • ABC by SKU: revenue is spread almost evenly (22 of 30 SKUs make up 78% of 2024 revenue), so it adds no insight.
  • Price elasticity: price and units sold have zero correlation in this data.
